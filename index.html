Hi! I’m a first-year graduate student in Physics at the Federal University of ABC (UFABC), Brazil. My research lies in the physics of strong gravitational fields, particularly black hole physics and the Penrose process.

Over the past years, I have engaged in five research projects, three of which focus directly on black holes, including general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics (GRMHD), the Blandford–Znajek process, and the geometry of Kerr spacetime. One of my primary motivations is to understand the role of magnetic fields in the dynamics and collimation of relativistic jets in the force-free region around black holes. I am particularly fascinated by the astrophysical environment close to the outer black hole horizon.

I’ve authored three papers on topics ranging from the shadow of charged wormholes to the geometry of rotating spacetimes. I’ve also presented my work at several national and international conferences.

My academic training includes advanced courses and summer schools in General Relativity, Classical Field Theory, Differential Geometry, and Magnetohydrodynamics. Currently, I am seeking opportunities to develop my knowledge in black hole astrophysics (jets, accretion disks, black hole magnetospheres and other phenomena near the hole) and GRMHD simulations.

Equatorial plane \(\theta = \pi/2\) seen from above; the hole spins counter-clockwise and \(G = c = 1\). Rays arrive from the right, \(b > 0\) is prograde, and the shaded band between the two critical \(b_{\rm c}\) is captured: the Kerr shadow is not symmetric. Short dashes: photon orbits; long dashes: ISCOs (prograde is always the inner circle). Hamilton's equations for \(H = \tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu\) in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates, integrated in Mino time \(d\tau = \Sigma\, d\lambda\) up to \(M\lambda_{\max}\); the integration stops at \(r_+ + 10^{-3}M\), where these coordinates become singular. Bound orbits (\(E < \mu\)) start at apoapsis.
